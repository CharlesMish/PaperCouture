## Design and construction judgment

The mitten recovery retains the square and shortens the palm by folding the
top behind it. Its side gates meet instead of stacking over one another. The
existing free thumb flap moves before the far side closes; no new squash,
swivel, cavity or unproved collapse is hidden in that step. Mirroring the
crease coordinates creates the other hand without mirroring its printed
texture. The diagnostic grid shows the true reverse on cuff/thumb and the
printed face on the palm. The 6 cm board comparison gives a more balanced
4.06 × 4.35 cm ornament than the tall original.

The low triangular thumb remains stylized. The main faces show no broad
intersection in the reviewed poses, but thin stacked-edge seams remain visible.
The rendered audit's 0.0165/0.022 endpoint-to-plane distances are not physical
penetration widths. Synthetic connector intersections are disclosed separately
from zero sampled material-facet crossings. These results justify an
Experimental candidate under the same standard as existing flat ornaments;
they do not certify practical finite-thickness paper folding.

Riso's correction makes its ends slightly busier while preserving the pink/teal
fade and useful proof margins. Crease bloom's corrected description now matches
the preserved crease-aligned artwork. Marbling remains a strong statement paper.
Dusk adds a visibly different broad tonal field, strongest on coat and skirt,
with correct original-material placement and a useful contrasting reverse.

## Integration safeguards and limits

descriptions, have no unreviewed attachment anchors and suggest 6 cm squares.

separate report; this reviewer did not independently rerun those broad browser
passes. No physical-paper or real-phone/Safari trial was performed. Sampled
numerical tests do not establish continuous collision freedom. No merge,
website import or publication was performed by this review.
