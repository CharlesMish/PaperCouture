# Fold choices and a first paper doll

## Where to ask the player

| Piece | Moment | Question to try |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cross-wrap top | After turning the sheet over, before crossing the first panel | **Which panel should lie on top?** Show both orders in a paper with one off-center flower. The second panel covers part of the first. |
| Boat-neck top | After the neckband and turn-over, before tapering the first side | **Keep the sides tapered or fold out short sleeves?** This is where Fable’s sketch first changes the crease geometry. The narrow shoulder crease needs to be in place before the sleeve can fold out. |
| Pleated skirt | At the existing depth choice, just before returning the inner edges | **How much lining should show between the pleats?** Preview the existing Shallow, Classic and Deep choices in the player’s paper. Sardine run puts red stripes in the channels, which makes their width easy to see. |

The cross-wrap is the smallest new fold to audition. In a local experiment I swapped just the two panel folds. Both orders passed the engine’s state checks and measured five hinge gaps at the sampled poses. The frontmost panel at the lower centre changed sides. The boat-neck top and skirt are follow-ups to [Fable’s findings](../../experiments/placement/findings.ts). The current [choice thumbnails](../../src/ui/shapeChoices.ts) show the finished geometry in two fixed colours.

## A first paper-doll study

Try an original **paper doll bookmark**. Lay a flat head-and-neck piece behind the studio’s folded kimono, add a separate dark paper hair piece and fold an obi for the waist. Let the maker slide the blossom onto a sleeve or crop it to a petal at the edge. Show the loose parts beside the assembled figure. In the board photograph, hide the outfit guide and see whether the doll still reads as a person. The kimono already has [credited construction notes](../kimono/NOTES.md) and a [printable folding sheet](../kimono/kimono-folding-sheet.pdf).

Once that figure reads, try the same head with the camp-collar shirt and pleated skirt from *Fish Market Saturday*. Can you recognize the person in a different outfit? That would give the rest of the wardrobe a place in the study.

For **Fold Along**, list the paper sizes, folds, cuts and joins someone needs at the table. A physical trial at those sizes would establish whether the bookmark holds together. For **Exhibition**, try the same figure with a turned head and one sleeve crossing the body. Show the layers in a second view so a player can see how the pose was made. Label the assembly as a digital study until there’s a worked paper example.

The Robert C. Williams Museum of Papermaking’s bookmark activity cuts hair and glues separate sleeves. Its notes also describe *shikishi ningyō*, figures composed into scenes on a rectangular backing. That suggests a second pinboard trial. Pin two versions of the same doll facing one another. Can a change in the sleeve alone make one appear to greet the other? Chrissy’s three-square doll offers a different construction reference, with two squares for the garment and one for the head.
