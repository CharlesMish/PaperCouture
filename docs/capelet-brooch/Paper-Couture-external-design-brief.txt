PAPER COUTURE — ONE STANDOUT FOLDED DESIGN
A reusable brief for Claude or Grok · 6 October 2026

Please develop ONE excellent garment or accessory for Paper Couture. We want a
convincing folded-paper object with a distinctive silhouette that belongs in a
small, coordinated wardrobe. Quality and coherence matter more than catalogue
size. Start with two or three concise concept comparisons, choose one, and
fully develop it. There is no minimum fold count: every fold should earn its
place through shape, structure, or a visible paper detail.

THE DESIGN
Explain the garment/accessory role, the intended front, back and side silhouette,
and what makes the design recognisable at outfit scale. A closure, opening,
collar, lapel or cuff must come from actual folded material. Do not paint a false
seam, invent a cut or hole, stretch a mesh, or substitute an attractive illustration
for buildable geometry. A front opening onto continuous lining is acceptable if
clearly described; it is not a through-hole or a wearable cavity.

One intact square is the preferred starting point. If a separate square is
essential, identify it explicitly and explain how the existing capture/attachment
workflow would support it. Avoid new engine machinery. Give a starting paper size
in centimetres, the resulting model/board dimensions, and show the piece beside
an existing top, bottom, hat and small accessory. Workshop close-ups auto-fit the
camera and cannot establish wardrobe scale. The current board has five slots;
a larger wardrobe or mannequin is outside this commission.

GEOMETRY AND PAPER
Use src/fold/engine.ts, timeline.ts and existing authored constructions. The
square spans [-1,1] in both material axes. A crease specifies two points, a
moving-side point, sense, and (only when valid) an existing flap tag. Coordinates
refer to the sheet at that step. Whole-stack valley folds and explicit turn-over
steps suit this workshop; a selective fold must really be connected and accessible.
Preserve all material, permanent UV coordinates, reversible motion and true
front/back textures. Return concrete ordered creases with named steps and useful
folding instructions, not just a final outline or an unconstrained shape slider.

Charlie particularly values moving a print to align blossom features after
folding. First prove the shape with existing neutral paper, then select an existing
complementary print or propose one restrained paper. Use actual-engine visibility
maps (scripts/paperLanding.ts) to explain which source areas appear on the front
and back. Show unshifted and deliberately aligned print placements at all four
quarter-turns. Keep paper generation separate from geometry; respect fixed versus
positionable paper settings. Avoid instructional-arrow-like decorative motifs.

EVIDENCE AND HANDOFF
Provide the implementation/diff, a short rationale, exact source revision and
reproducible commands. Run Node24 setup, typecheck, tests and production build.
Add focused checks for retained area, connectivity, rigid facets, endpoint
continuity, forward/back/reset and reversal. Keep the established layer/floor
limits; sample motion for triangle piercing at least 81 times per operation.
Record failures and limitations instead of relaxing the gates to pass a design.

Show actual app renders: front, angle, back, revealing intermediate folds,
print before/after alignment, and a complete outfit at desktop, 390px and 320px.
Check clipping and composite PNG, saved-capture preservation, remove/Undo and
reload. Mark new work Experimental until owner review. Automated engine/browser
checks do not certify continuous collision-free motion, physical foldability or
real-phone feel. Say whether anyone actually folded paper or used a physical
phone; suggest a paper prototype where it would resolve an uncertainty.

Return one developed candidate, its strongest comparison images, test results,
and any unresolved compromises. Do not contact anyone or publish the result.
