# Refine 2 — what the sheets show

Captures are one Playwright Chromium session, software WebGL, viewport 900×680, of the production build. Every front used camera `[0, 1.589, 5.271]`. Every back used `[-0, 1.589, -5.271]`. Workshop and display controls are hidden after layout, so the paper name, Turn paper, and the Front / Angle / Back buttons are not in these frames. The upper corners are room grey. The stand and the floor are in the frame. This is not a phone and not a physical sheet.

Before tiles are `542d7d1`. After tiles are this revision, with the reverse turning together with the front. `before-after-fronts.png` is the finished dress at the Display front. `reverse-garden-orientations.png` adds the before row at the same four turns. `rotation-grid.png` is the after collection: six fronts, then the backs of Woven checks and Reverse garden.

## Midnight orchard

The before front has several separate brown limbs and round fruits with a pale spot on each. The after front has one limb. It is thicker at the base and tapers toward the tip. Two short side branches leave it, curve downward, and end in fruit. The other fruits hang against the limb on short stems that bend down and taper. There is no bare spur. The fruits stay round: the gold one slightly wider, the lower apricot slightly taller, the persimmon a little flatter across the top where the calyx sits, each with a small stem notch and a darker flat tone along the bottom. No pale spot. Deep blue ground, ochre collar and sleeves, and a bare blue hem are unchanged. One fruit still sits in the skirt.

At 90° the fruits sit more to one side. At 180° they are higher and the lower skirt is blue. At 270° they sit high, toward the collar, and the hem is blue. Those quieter hems are the same drawing turned.

## Tidal bands

The bands are the same curves and the same order as the before front. At 0° the front is pale, teal, cream, then the hem green, and the collar is the deep teal reverse. The pale band reads a little lighter than the before front. At 90° the colours sit side by side. At 180° the green is up under the collar and the pale band is low. At 270° they sit side by side the other way. No extra band or outline.

## Plum scatter

The before front repeats similar petal clusters. The after front has one large plum cluster on the bodice, a closed bud above it, a small mark on the left of the skirt, a small coral mark on the right, and a more open coral cluster lower down. Blush ground separates them. At 90° and 270° one cluster remains and most of the skirt is blush. At 180° the drift is flipped. Those quieter turns were left quiet.

## Cut-paper mosaic

Unchanged. The after front matches the before front. The skirt is mostly terracotta, with warm ground along the right side. At 90° terracotta is high and oxblood is low. At 180° oxblood is at the shoulder and rose runs along the hem. At 270° rose and oxblood are high and terracotta is low. The gaps stay gaps.

## Woven checks

The before front has a light strip along the blue bands and along the ochre bands. The after front has no light strip. Ochre and blue bands still cross, six across, and the check still reaches the hem. At each crossing one band runs straight through and the other stops short: a darker flat tone of the same colour, then a narrow ground gap. The collar and the sleeves stay slate. At 90° the same weave is turned. The back is slate in the centre, with the weave on both lower sides; turning moves that weave.

## Reverse garden

The before front is mist at 0°, 90°, 180°, and 270°. The collar keeps a small pale sprig, and the sleeves keep partial warm marks. The four before backs match one another: the reverse did not turn.

The after front keeps that collar sprig at 0°, and adds one thin stem on the lower left, with leaves and two buds. The centre and the right side stay mist. At 90° the stem is on the right side of the skirt. At 180° the front body is mist again, and the collar carries a small blossom. At 270° the collar is plain green and the stem lies along the hem. The sleeves keep partial blossoms. The after backs are not the same picture: at 0° the flowering stem runs down the centre; at 90°, 180°, and 270° that stem and the corner blossoms have turned, and the front stem shows on the lower sides when it has come around.

The sheet drawing was not moved to follow a turn. The 180° collar blossom is the lower reverse flower turned into the collar band. It still reads as a bud, so it was left where it was drawn. Plain collars at 90° and 270° are turns of the same sheet.

## Preference, separate from defects

Nothing in these frames is a broken fold or a torn motif. These are preferences about the drawings:

1. Midnight orchard at 0°. The fruits read as one branch. The hem is still bare blue. 180° and 270° leave more blue skirt.
2. Tidal bands stay broad. A quarter turn sets them side by side.
3. Plum scatter at 90° and 270° leaves most of the skirt blush.
4. Cut-paper mosaic. A quarter turn changes which field owns the skirt.
5. Woven checks. The over-and-under read is the point of the print. The slate reverse is quiet on purpose.
6. Reverse garden. The front stays mostly mist. The garden is on the reverse, so some turns show more of it on the collar and the back than others.
